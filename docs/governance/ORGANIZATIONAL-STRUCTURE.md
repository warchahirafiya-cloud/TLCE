# TLCE — Proposed Organizational Structure & Program Governance

**Status:** Proposed operating model — not a legal organization chart  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**Parent identity:** TLCE — The Global Institution of Excellence  
**Founding name:** The Last Cosmic Explosion

## 1. Purpose and status

This document translates the TLCE institutional vision into a practical program structure. It defines proposed functions, boundaries, decision rights, and maturity gates.

It does **not** establish a legal entity, appoint officers, create subsidiaries, establish accreditation, or claim that any proposed unit is staffed or operational. Until legal formation and appointments are independently completed, all units below are internal programs or capabilities under the TLCE parent initiative.

## 2. Organizational model

TLCE uses a federated, program-based model:

- The **Institutional Office** maintains strategy, identity, governance, portfolio priorities, risk oversight, and external institutional communications.
- **Programs and laboratories** own domain roadmaps, delivery quality, technical or academic standards, and evidence of outcomes.
- **Shared enabling functions** provide security, privacy, architecture, operations, finance planning, legal coordination, and communications standards.
- **Products and platforms** deliver specific user-facing capabilities under documented ownership and service boundaries.

## 3. Unit mandates and boundaries

### 3.1 Institutional Office

**Mandate:** Maintain the institutional direction and ensure consistent, responsible execution.

**Responsibilities:**
- approve institutional strategy and annual priorities;
- maintain the program portfolio and decision log;
- set organization-wide quality, security, privacy, and ethics baselines;
- review major risks, partnerships, public claims, and resource commitments;
- resolve cross-program conflicts and authorize stage transitions.

**Boundary:** The Institutional Office does not bypass technical owners, security gates, legal review, or repository change controls.

### 3.2 TLCE Research

**Mandate:** Conduct and coordinate research, evaluation, and evidence generation across the institution's domains.

**Responsibilities:** research questions, methods, reproducibility, evaluation protocols, publication review, data stewardship, and research integrity.

**Boundary:** Research findings must distinguish validated results from hypotheses, demonstrations, and preliminary observations.

### 3.3 TLCE Academy

**Mandate:** Develop learning resources and structured capability-building initiatives.

**Responsibilities:** learning outcomes, curriculum design, instructional quality, accessibility, assessment, and learner feedback.

**Boundary:** Do not imply accreditation, degree-awarding authority, or recognized qualifications unless separately authorized and verified in the relevant jurisdiction.

### 3.4 TLCE AI LAB

**Mandate:** Develop and validate AI systems, agentic workflows, evaluation infrastructure, and supporting engineering capabilities.

**Boundary:** AI LAB repositories and infrastructure remain independent from TLCE.store and TLCE-UI-LAB. Shared identity or strategy does not grant cross-repository access, data sharing, or deployment authority. Existing security and approval controls remain in force.

### 3.5 TLCE Innovation

**Mandate:** Turn validated research and identified needs into responsibly tested prototypes, partnerships, and new capabilities.

**Responsibilities:** opportunity assessment, prototype governance, feasibility, user validation, risk review, and transition recommendations.

**Boundary:** A prototype is not a production service; funding, commercial commitments, and production launch require separate approval.

### 3.6 TLCE.store

**Mandate:** Operate as a digital product/platform initiative in the TLCE ecosystem.

**Boundary:** Its current legal, commercial, operational, and technical status must be represented accurately. The institutional structure does not imply company registration or authorize new payment, hosting, or paid-service commitments.

### 3.7 TLCE-UI-LAB

**Mandate:** Explore and validate user-interface patterns, accessibility, interaction systems, and design prototypes.

**Boundary:** It remains a dedicated UI research/prototyping workspace and does not become the owner of the parent platform's core contracts.

### 3.8 QuestPulse

**Mandate:** Deliver a product module within the TLCE parent platform.

**Boundary:** QuestPulse is not a separate parent institution or standalone parent application. It must use approved TLCE platform contracts and security boundaries.

## 4. Shared enabling functions

These functions may initially be performed by the same small team or designated owner; naming them does not imply dedicated staff exist.

| Function | Minimum responsibility |
|---|---|
| Architecture & Engineering Governance | Architecture decisions, interface contracts, technical review, release gates |
| Security & Privacy | Threat modelling, access control, data lifecycle, incident readiness |
| Quality & Evaluation | Test strategy, evidence standards, performance and reliability measures |
| Legal & Compliance Coordination | Jurisdictional review, contracts, IP, regulatory and trademark checks |
| Finance & Resource Planning | Cost estimates, budget controls, funding assumptions, no unapproved spend |
| Communications & Partnerships | Accurate public claims, partner diligence, publication and brand consistency |

## 5. Decision rights

| Decision | Accountable authority | Required consultation / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional mission, identity, strategic priorities | Institutional founder / authorized governing body | Written rationale, impact and legal review where relevant |
| Program scope and roadmap | Program lead (once appointed) | Portfolio alignment, capacity and risk review |
| Architecture and technical standards | Designated architecture owner | ADR, threat model, compatibility and tests |
| Security exception | Designated security authority | Written risk acceptance, scope, expiry and compensating controls |
| Public release / production deployment | Authorized release owner | CI evidence, security gates, rollback plan and explicit approval |
| Paid commitments and contracts | Authorized financial/legal signatory | Cost, funding, legal review and explicit approval |
| Public institutional or accreditation claims | Authorized institutional communications owner | Documentary evidence and jurisdictional/legal verification |

No role or person is deemed appointed by this document. Until roles are formally assigned, decisions remain with the currently authorized project owner and existing repository controls.

## 6. Program lifecycle and maturity gates

Each program progresses through explicit stages:

1. **Concept** — problem, intended users, rationale, and exclusions documented.
2. **Discovery** — user needs, feasibility, risks, alternatives, and success measures assessed.
3. **Prototype** — isolated proof of concept with non-production data and bounded access.
4. **Validated pilot** — tests, user evidence, security/privacy review, operating-cost model, and rollback plan completed.
5. **Operational service** — named owner, support process, monitoring, incident response, service objectives, and approved funding established.
6. **Scale / partnership** — independent evidence of demand, capacity, legal readiness, governance, and measurable outcomes.
7. **Retirement** — migration, data retention/deletion, user communication, and access revocation completed.

A program must not claim a later maturity stage without the corresponding evidence.

## 7. Minimum program charter

Every program must maintain:
- purpose, scope, exclusions, and intended beneficiaries;
- accountable owner and delegated decision rights;
- roadmap, dependencies, and measurable outcomes;
- architecture, data classification, and security model;
- privacy, retention, deletion, and incident requirements;
- budget, recurring cost, and funding assumptions;
- quality/evaluation plan and release gates;
- risks, legal constraints, and review cadence;
- exit, transfer, and retirement plan.

## 8. Operating cadence

A lightweight cadence is recommended, adjusted to actual capacity:
- **Weekly:** delivery, blockers, security/quality risks, and next actions.
- **Monthly:** portfolio review, cost exposure, evidence, and priority changes.
- **Quarterly:** strategy, program maturity, partnerships, and risk posture.
- **Before every release:** CI/security evidence, owner approval, rollback readiness, and public-claim review.

Meetings are not mandatory where there is no active team; written decision records may be used instead.

## 9. Initial implementation sequence

1. Approve the institutional identity charter and this proposed operating model.
2. Name accountable owners only when roles, authority, and capacity are clear.
3. Create one-page charters for active programs, beginning with TLCE AI LAB and TLCE.store.
4. Maintain a portfolio register with status labels: proposed, active, paused, operational, or retired.
5. Establish shared security, privacy, quality, and cost-review checklists.
6. Review legal structure, jurisdiction, name and trademark availability before external institutional representations.
7. Publish only verified program capabilities and current maturity.

## 10. Change control

This is a documentation-only governance proposal. It does not authorize:
- creating or merging repositories;
- changing default branches or production code;
- deploying services or changing DNS;
- provisioning secrets, cloud resources, paid services, or accounts;
- sharing private data or credentials between programs;
- making legal, accreditation, partnership, or funding commitments.

All technical changes remain subject to their own feature branches, reviews, tests, and explicit release approvals.
